5 years ago I built my first dashboard. I pulled data, build dashboard, presented numbers.

I walked into that meeting proud of every chart, every number, every filter.

Everyone nodded. Nothing changed.

That's when it hit me — I had presented data. Not insights. Not recommendations.



And there's a world of difference.

Here's the framework that changed everything for me:

**Step 1** — Data is the technical foundation. 

**Step 2** — Insight is where real analysis happens. 

**Step 3** — Recommendations is where you create business impact.



Step 1 is learnable. AI can already automate most of it.

Steps 2 and 3? That's human intelligence. That comes from experience, business sense and judgment. No tool replaces that.



### ***Step 1 — DATA The foundation. And the most critical step to get right.***



Before any analysis happens you need data that is clean, structured, accurate and accessible.

This means connecting to sources — databases, CRMs, marketing platforms, product event trackers — build and maintain ETL pipelines, automated data flows and dashboards that make numbers consumable for the business.



Data quality is non-negotiable. If your pipeline has errors, if your sources are inconsistent — everything built on top is misleading.



But data quality is only half of what makes this step critical.



This is also the stage where you build your business foundation — and this is what separates analysts who build dashboards from analysts who deliver insights and recommendations.



**At this stage an analyst needs to deeply understand:**

**— The business and ecosystem** — how does the company make money, who are the users, what does the competitive landscape look like, what are the internal and external forces at play

**— The metrics and KPIs that actually matter** — not every number in your database is worth tracking. Which ones drive decisions? Which ones reflect real business health? Which ones are vanity metrics that look good but mean nothing? And critically — metric definitions must be aligned across teams. If sales and product are measuring conversion differently, your analysis will never match theirs.

**— Internal benchmarks** — what does "good" look like for this business specifically? A 2% conversion rate might be excellent for one company and terrible for another. You need to know the baseline before you can identify what's above or below it

**— What parameters to track** — before you build anything you need to know what questions the business is trying to answer. What are we optimizing for? What does success look like? What are the guardrail metrics we cannot compromise?



This groundwork done at the data stage is what sets up everything that follows. The quality of your insights in step 2 and the strength of your recommendations in step 3 are directly proportional to how well you understood the business in step 1.



You cannot find meaningful patterns in data if you don't know what meaningful looks like for that business.



What most people think: pulling data and building a dashboard IS the analyst's job. Done.



What it actually is: just step one. The starting line not the finish line.

A dashboard shows you WHAT happened. It doesn't tell you WHY. It doesn't tell you what to do. It's a window into the data — not the analysis itself.

The real work starts after the dashboard is built.



Next up — Step 2: Insight. Where the real analysis begins and where most analysts either shine or stay stuck.
